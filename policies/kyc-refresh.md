# KYC Refresh for Existing Customers

> Synthetic document written for the Secure Multi-Agent Delegation project. The bank, its rules and its numbers are invented. Not a real bank's policy and not legal or regulatory advice.

## When KYC expires

A customer's KYC (know your customer) record expires when its refresh date passes: every 2 years for high-risk
customers, every 8 years for medium-risk and every 10 years for low-risk. The customer record then shows status
**expired**.

## What the teller must do

For every customer at their branch whose KYC has expired, the teller:

1. Contacts the customer within **7 business days** by phone or SMS, and by letter if there is no answer.
2. Asks for a current identity document and proof of address, or a self-declaration if nothing has changed.
3. Verifies the documents in person or through the bank's video KYC process, and updates the record the same day.
4. Notes each attempt on the customer record.

If the customer cannot be reached after three attempts over 30 days, the teller refers the case to the Branch
Manager.

## Restrictions until KYC is refreshed

While KYC is expired:

- No new account, card, loan or other product may be opened for the customer.
- Withdrawals and outgoing transfers are limited to **Rs 10,000 per day**; credits into the account are allowed.
- Address or phone-number changes are not accepted until the refresh is complete.

The restrictions lift as soon as the record is updated to verified.

## What staff must not do

Staff never accept documents by personal email or messaging apps, and never mark a record verified without seeing
the documents.
