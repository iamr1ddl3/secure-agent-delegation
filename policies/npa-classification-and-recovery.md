# NPA Classification and Recovery

> Synthetic document written for the Secure Multi-Agent Delegation project. The bank, its rules and its numbers are invented. Not a real bank's policy and not legal or regulatory advice.

## When a loan becomes an NPA

A loan is classified as a non-performing asset (NPA) on the day an instalment is **more than 90 days past due**.
Classification is automatic and is never delayed by a promise to pay, a part payment that does not clear all
arrears, or a request from the branch.

## Upgrading an NPA

An NPA returns to standard only when all arrears (instalments, interest and charges) are paid in full. A
restructured NPA stays classified as NPA until it has been serviced without a miss for 12 months.

## Who owns recovery

On classification, ownership moves from the Branch Manager to the **Regional Recovery Unit** of the branch's region.
The Branch Manager keeps the customer relationship and supports the unit, but may not agree new terms alone.

## Recovery steps

1. Within 7 days: recovery notice to the borrower and any guarantor, stating the arrears and the options.
2. Within 30 days: the Regional Recovery Unit assesses whether a restructuring (loan-restructuring) or a
   settlement is realistic.
3. Secured loans (home, auto, gold, business with collateral): if no plan is agreed within 60 days of
   classification, the unit may start recovery against the security under applicable law, after legal review.
4. Unsecured loans (personal): the unit may refer the account to the bank's approved recovery agency; agencies
   must follow collections-contact-rules.
5. Write-off is decided by the Credit Committee only, never by a branch or region.

## Audit

Internal Audit reviews a sample of NPA files every quarter: classification date, notices sent, and that no waiver
was granted outside fee-waiver-authority.
